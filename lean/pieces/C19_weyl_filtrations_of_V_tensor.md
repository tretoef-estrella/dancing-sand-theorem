# The Weyl filtrations of `V ⊗ Δ_A(m)` and `V ⊗ ∇_A(m)` (Lemma 2.4)

Source: *The Dancing Sand Theorem* (R. Amichis Luengo, 2026, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23188299), §2.3, Lemma 2.4. This is the third piece of stage 1c. It reuses piece C18 (`RequestProject/DPLattice/`): `DPLattice`, `DPLattice.IsDP`, `DPLattice.H`, `DPLattice.Hom`, `DPLattice.one`, `DPLattice.V`, `DPLattice.tensorV`, `DPLattice.bvec`, `DPLattice.delta`, `DPLattice.nabla`, `DPLattice.T5_delta_one`, `DPLattice.T5_nabla_one`.

The paper writes `Δ_A(m) ⊗ V`; since C18 builds `V ⊗ M` (the factor `V` first), this piece states Lemma 2.4 for `V ⊗ Δ_A(m)` and `V ⊗ ∇_A(m)`, which is the form used later (`T(2l+2) = V ⊗ Φ(T(l))`). The maps are given explicitly, so no duality and no argument over `ℚ` is needed, and everything holds over every commutative ring `A`.

## Setting

`A` is a commutative ring. Notation of C18: in `Δ_A(λ)` the basis is `e_r` (`0 ≤ r ≤ λ`), in `∇_A(λ)` it is `w_r`; in `V ⊗ M` the vector with `m` in slot `e` is `b_e m` (`bvec e m`). Write `e_r := 0` and `w_r := 0` when `r < 0` or `r > λ`.

**Morphisms.** A morphism `M → N` of DP-lattices is C18's `Hom M N` (an `A`-linear map commuting with every `F^{(r)}`, every `E^{(s)}` and `H`). Define the identity `Hom.id M` and the composition `Hom.comp g f`.

**Short exact sequence.** Three DP-lattices `X, Y, Z` and morphisms `f : X → Y`, `g : Y → Z` form a *short exact sequence* if `f` is injective, `g` is surjective and `range f = ker g` (as `A`-submodules of `Y`). Since `Z` is free over `A`, this makes `f(X)` a saturated sublattice of `Y` with `Y/f(X) ≅ Z`.

**The four maps** (for `m ≥ 1`), defined on basis vectors and extended `A`-linearly:
- `fΔ : Δ_A(m+1) → V ⊗ Δ_A(m)`, `e'_R ↦ b_0 e_R + b_1 e_{R−1}` (`0 ≤ R ≤ m+1`);
- `gΔ : V ⊗ Δ_A(m) → Δ_A(m−1)`, `b_1 e_s ↦ e''_s` (`0 ≤ s ≤ m−1`), `b_1 e_m ↦ 0`, `b_0 e_0 ↦ 0`, `b_0 e_R ↦ −e''_{R−1}` (`1 ≤ R ≤ m`);
- `f∇ : ∇_A(m−1) → V ⊗ ∇_A(m)`, `w''_r ↦ b_0 w_{r+1} − b_1 w_r` (`0 ≤ r ≤ m−1`);
- `g∇ : V ⊗ ∇_A(m) → ∇_A(m+1)`, `b_0 w_r ↦ w'_r`, `b_1 w_r ↦ w'_{r+1}` (`0 ≤ r ≤ m`).

(Here `e'`, `w'` are the bases of the lattices with parameter `m+1`, and `e''`, `w''` those with parameter `m−1`.)

## Part M — the maps are morphisms

**(M1)** `fΔ`, `gΔ`, `f∇`, `g∇` commute with every `F^{(r)}`, every `E^{(s)}` and `H`; so they are morphisms of DP-lattices.
**(M2)** `Hom.id` and `Hom.comp` are morphisms, and composition is associative with `Hom.id` as unit.

## Part S — Lemma 2.4

**(S1)** For every `m ≥ 1`, `Δ_A(m+1) —fΔ→ V ⊗ Δ_A(m) —gΔ→ Δ_A(m−1)` is a short exact sequence.
**(S2)** For every `m ≥ 1`, `∇_A(m−1) —f∇→ V ⊗ ∇_A(m) —g∇→ ∇_A(m+1)` is a short exact sequence.
**(S3)** `f(e'_0) = b_0 e_0`: the image of `fΔ` is the sublattice generated by `b_0 e_0`, that is, `range fΔ` is the `A`-span of the vectors `F^{(R)}(b_0 e_0)` (`0 ≤ R ≤ m+1`).
**(S4)** (`m = 0`) `V ⊗ Δ_A(0) ≅ V` and `V ⊗ ∇_A(0) ≅ V` as DP-lattices: there are morphisms both ways whose composites are the identities. (With C18 (T5), `V = Δ_A(1) = ∇_A(1)`.)

*Proof sketch.* (M1) on basis vectors, from the formulas of C18: for `fΔ` and `F^{(t)}`, `C(R+t−1, t−1) + C(R+t−1, t) = C(R+t, t)`; for `gΔ` and `E^{(s)}`, `C(m−s'+s, s) − C(m−s'+s−1, s−1) = C(m−1−s'+s, s)`; for `f∇` and `F`, `(m−r−1)b_0 w_{r+2} + b_1 w_{r+1} − (m−r) b_1 w_{r+1}`; for `g∇`, `C(m, R) + C(m, R−1) = C(m+1, R)`; and the same Pascal-type identities for the divided powers. (S1), (S2): injectivity and surjectivity are triangular on bases ordered by weight; `g ∘ f = 0` on bases; and `ker g ⊆ range f` directly: for `y = Σ_{R=0}^{m} (c_R b_0 e_R + d_R b_1 e_R)` with `gΔ y = 0`, the coefficients satisfy `d_s = c_{s+1}` for `s < m`, and then `y = fΔ(Σ_{R=0}^{m} c_R e'_R + d_m e'_{m+1})` (and the same bookkeeping for `∇`, with `y = f∇(Σ_r a_r w''_r)`). This works over every commutative ring; no rank argument is used.
