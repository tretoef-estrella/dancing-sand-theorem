## Setting

`B` is a commutative ring with `h2 : (2 : B) = 0`. The case of the paper is `B = 𝔽_2 = ZMod 2`. Isomorphism means `Doubling.Iso`: `Hom`s both ways whose composites are `Hom.id`.

Notation for bases:
- `V = DPLattice.V B` has basis `b_0, b_1` (`Fin 2`), weights `1, −1`.
- `V^{⊗3} := tensorV (tensorV (V B))` has basis `Fin 2 × (Fin 2 × Fin 2)`. Write the basis vector `δ_{(e_1, (e_2, e_3))}` as the word `e_1e_2e_3`; its weight is `wtV e_1 + wtV e_2 + wtV e_3`.
- `Δ(3) := delta B 3` has basis `e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3` (`Fin 4`), weights `3, 1, −1, −3`, with `F^{(t)} e_r = C(r + t, t) e_{r+t}` and `E^{(s)} e_r = C(3 − r + s, s) e_{r−s}`.
- `V ⊗ V^{[1]} := tensorV (Twist.frob h2 (V B))` has basis `Fin 2 × Fin 2`; write `δ_{(e, e')}` as `b_e b'_{e'}`. Its weights are `wtV e + 2·wtV e'`.

## Part S — the direct sum of DP-lattices

For DP-lattices `M`, `N` over any commutative ring `A`, define `M ⊕ N` (`dsum M N`) by:
- basis `M.ι ⊕ N.ι` (`Sum`), weights `Sum.elim M.wt N.wt`;
- `F^{(r)}` acting blockwise: `(F^{(r)} x) ∘ inl = M.Fd r (x ∘ inl)` and `(F^{(r)} x) ∘ inr = N.Fd r (x ∘ inr)`; likewise `E^{(r)}`.

**(S1)** `M ⊕ N` satisfies `IsDP`. So `dsum M N : DPLattice A`.

**(S2)** The inclusions `M → M ⊕ N`, `N → M ⊕ N` (`x ↦ Sum.elim x 0`, `y ↦ Sum.elim 0 y`) and the projections `M ⊕ N → M`, `M ⊕ N → N` (`z ↦ z ∘ inl`, `z ↦ z ∘ inr`) are `Hom`s. For `f : Hom M M'` and `g : Hom N N'`, the blockwise map `f ⊕ g` is a `Hom (M ⊕ N) (M' ⊕ N')`. It sends `(Hom.id, Hom.id)` to `Hom.id` and composites to composites.

## Part L — Lemma 3.5 (first sentence)

**(L1) `V^{⊗3} ≅ Δ(3) ⊕ (V ⊕ V)`.** Let `D := dsum (delta B 3) (dsum (V B) (V B))`. Its basis is `Fin 4 ⊕ (Fin 2 ⊕ Fin 2)`. Write `e_r` for `inl r`, `b_e` for `inr (inl e)` (first copy of `V`), and `c_e` for `inr (inr e)` (second copy).

The map `g : D → V^{⊗3}` is defined on basis vectors by:
- `e_0 ↦ 000`;
- `e_1 ↦ 100 + 010 + 001`;
- `e_2 ↦ 110 + 101 + 011`;
- `e_3 ↦ 111`;
- `b_0 ↦ 100 + 010`;
- `b_1 ↦ 101 + 011`;
- `c_0 ↦ 010 + 001`;
- `c_1 ↦ 110 + 101`.

The map `h : V^{⊗3} → D` is defined on basis vectors by:
- `000 ↦ e_0`;
- `001 ↦ e_1 + b_0`;
- `010 ↦ e_1 + b_0 + c_0`;
- `100 ↦ e_1 + c_0`;
- `011 ↦ e_2 + c_1`;
- `101 ↦ e_2 + b_1 + c_1`;
- `110 ↦ e_2 + b_1`;
- `111 ↦ e_3`.

Prove that, when `2 = 0` in `B`:
- `g` and `h` are `Hom`s;
- `h ∘ g = Hom.id` and `g ∘ h = Hom.id`.

So `Nonempty (Doubling.Iso (tensorV (tensorV (V B))) D)` holds, and `g` sends `e_r` to `F^{(r)}(000)`.

(Meaning: `e_r ↦ F^{(r)} b_0^{⊗3}` is the copy of `Δ(3)` generated by `b_0^{⊗3}`. The vectors `p_1 = 100 + 010` and `p_2 = 010 + 001` span the weight-1 part of its orthogonal complement, `E p_i = 0`, and `{p_i, F p_i}` spans a copy of `V`.)

**(L2) `Δ(3) ≅ V ⊗ V^{[1]}`.** The map `u : Δ(3) → V ⊗ V^{[1]}` is defined on basis vectors by:
- `e_0 ↦ b_0 b'_0`;
- `e_1 ↦ b_1 b'_0`;
- `e_2 ↦ b_0 b'_1`;
- `e_3 ↦ b_1 b'_1`.

Its inverse `u⁻¹` is the reverse bijection of bases. Prove that, when `2 = 0` in `B`:
- `u` and `u⁻¹` are `Hom`s;
- they are mutually inverse;
- `u (e_r) = F^{(r)}(b_0 b'_0)` for `r = 0, 1, 2, 3`.

**(L3) The case of the paper.** Both isomorphisms exist for `B = ZMod 2`.

*Proof sketch.*
- **(S1).** Every axiom holds blockwise. For (W) and (K) on `δ_{inl i}`, the computation stays in the `M` block.
- **(L1), (L2).** The matrices of `g`, `h`, `u`, `u⁻¹` have entries `0, 1`. Commutation with `F^{(r)}`, `E^{(r)}` is an identity among integer matrices, true modulo `2`: compare both sides on each basis vector. For `r ≥ 4`, both sides vanish: every weight lies in `[−3, 3]`. Commutation with `H` holds because every basis vector goes to a combination of basis vectors of the same weight.
- Over `ℤ` the maps are not `Hom`s: `E^{(1)}(100 + 010) = 2·000` while `E^{(1)} b_0 = 0` in `V`. So `h2` is used.
